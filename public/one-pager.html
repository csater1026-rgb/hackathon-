AI gave everyone access to answers. Almost no one has access to a teacher. For a kid with none, that gap is the whole problem — so we built the teacher, not another answer box.

Who we built it for

Marcus, 15.

A curious student at a rural high school with no computer-science teacher and no one to ask when he gets stuck. He has a locked-down school Chromebook, patchy wifi, and real curiosity about code and AI — and every door into the subject assumes a teacher he doesn't have.

The barrier we remove

"There's no one to ask when I get stuck — and the whole field is invisible from where I'm standing."

The barrier isn't a lack of access to AI. It's that access to answers is not access to a teacher — and that the fields beyond "learn to code" (security, networking, quantum) have no on-ramp for someone learning alone.

How it works

🎓 A Socratic teacher

It withholds finished answers on purpose — asks what you tried, gives escalating hints, and lets you steer with “nudge / really stuck / I’ve got it.” A teacher makes you think.

🗺️ The whole field

Nine guided tracks (46 lessons): programming, cybersecurity, networking, how computers work, databases, AI, algorithms, game dev, quantum. The rooms no one shows you exist.

🧠 It remembers you

Tell it your name and interests once; it weaves your world into examples and links new ideas to lessons you’ve finished across every field. A teacher knows you.

🎤 You can talk to it

It reads replies aloud and you can answer with your voice — built into the browser, no cost. A teacher speaks — and it’s a reading/typing bridge.

⌨️ Run real code, on a Chromebook

A Code Lab runs actual Python in the browser with nothing to install, and hands your errors straight to the teacher. A teacher lets you do it.

✨ See it, then touch it

Hand-drawn diagrams show the shape of an idea — how a website loads, a neural network, packets — and live widgets let you flip binary bits, run a cipher, or measure a qubit. Some ideas only click when you touch them.

📚 Then it sends you further

Every field hands you a shortlist of the best free courses — freeCodeCamp, CS50, Elements of AI, IBM Quantum — flagging the ones that grant a real certificate. A teacher opens the next door.

🌍 Works where the student is

No login, no install, nothing to buy. Progress saves in the browser, so it loads on a locked-down school Chromebook. The door is actually open.

Not a destination — a doorway

Why it matters

Most schools never open the door.

A huge share of schools still offer no real computer-science class — and even fewer show students the careers behind it: security analyst, network engineer, data scientist, game developer. You can't choose a path you were never shown exists. For these students, the subject and every career in it simply stays invisible.

A $200 Chromebook is now enough.

Because it needs no install, no account, and no admin rights, the locked-down school Chromebook — the one device almost every student does have — can run real Python and explore security, networking, AI, and quantum. The cheapest, most restricted machine in the building just became a doorway into all of it.
