◆ CLASS OF ONE · the teacher you never had

You never had a computer science teacher. Now you do.

A patient teacher who meets you on any computer, tablet, or phone you already own, opens a whole field you never knew was there, and keeps pointing you further into ideas so enjoyable you will want to keep learning them.

A computer A phone A tablet Anywhere with a browser

Who we built it for

Marcus, 15.

A curious student at a rural high school with no computer science teacher and no one to ask when he gets stuck. He has an old school Chromebook that will not let him install anything, patchy wifi, and real curiosity about code and AI. Every door into the subject assumes a teacher he simply does not have.

The barrier we remove

"There's no one to ask when I get stuck, and the whole field is invisible from where I'm standing."

The barrier is not a lack of access to AI. It is that access to answers is not the same as access to a teacher, and that fields beyond learning to code, like security, networking, and quantum, have no clear way in for someone learning alone.

What changes when you have one

An answer machine
you: my code has an error
> here's the fixed code:
for i in range(10):
    print(i)
>
You copy it. It works. You did not really learn it.
A teacher
"Before I say anything, what did you expect to happen, and what actually happened? Even the exact error helps. Let's look together."
You figure it out yourself. It sticks.

A teacher does not just answer. A teacher waits, asks good questions, and helps you find it yourself, so the next time you get stuck, you already know what to try.

How it works

A Socratic teacher

It withholds finished answers on purpose, asks what you tried, and gives you a nudge instead of the whole solution. A teacher makes you think.

A whole new world

Nine guided fields, forty six lessons: programming, cybersecurity, networking, how computers work, databases, AI, algorithms, game development, and quantum. Rooms no one ever showed you.

It remembers you

Tell it your name and interests once, and it weaves your world into examples and connects new ideas to lessons you already finished. A teacher knows you.

You can talk to it

It reads replies aloud, and you can answer with your voice instead of typing, built right into the browser. A teacher speaks with you.

Real code, anywhere

A Code Lab runs actual Python right in the browser with nothing to install, and hands your errors straight to the teacher. A teacher lets you do it.

See it, then touch it

Clear diagrams show the shape of an idea, and live widgets let you flip binary bits, run a cipher, or measure a qubit yourself. Some ideas only click when you touch them.

A path to keep going

Finish a field and a roadmap appears: projects to build, topics to learn next, free courses with real certificates, and the careers this can lead to. A teacher shows you where the road goes.

Open to anyone

No login, no install, nothing to buy. Progress saves right on your device, whatever that device happens to be. The door is actually open.

A pathway, not a single lesson

This was never about answering one question. It takes a student who felt locked out and walks them into a whole new world, then keeps pointing forward, toward ideas so satisfying to finally understand that you want to keep going.

Why it matters

Most schools never open the door.

A huge share of schools still offer no real computer science class, and even fewer show students the careers behind it: security analyst, network engineer, data scientist, game developer. You cannot choose a path you were never shown exists. For these students, the subject and every career inside it simply stays invisible.

Any screen you own is enough.

It needs no install, no account, and no admin rights, so it runs on a school Chromebook, a family computer, a borrowed tablet, or the phone already in your pocket. The device does not get to decide who is allowed to learn this anymore.

Required disclosures

AI tools: Google Gemini (gemini-3.6-flash, called through an API that matches OpenAI's format) as the live teacher; Claude Code as a coding assistant during the build.

Datasets: none. No training, scraped, or collected data. All lesson content and teaching instructions were written by us.

Other: Pyodide (an open source build of Python that runs inside WebAssembly) powers the Code Lab.
